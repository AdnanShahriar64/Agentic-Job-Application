# Personal Motivation Library

**Purpose:** Authoritative, reusable source for authentic first-person cover letters. This file expresses motivations, not a résumé or a list of qualifications. Cover-letter agents may paraphrase the ideas naturally but must not invent motivations or claim project outcomes unsupported by experience libraries.

## MOT-001 — Primary motivation: Physics-based modeling from first principles

**Status:** Explicitly confirmed by candidate

**Statement:** My primary engineering motivation is developing mathematical and physics-based models from first principles.

**Confirmed deeper motivation:** What makes this work rewarding to me is understanding the underlying physics and mathematics. The satisfaction comes from making sense of the governing principles, not merely obtaining a numerical output.

**Meaning for cover letters:** Lead with intellectual curiosity about the underlying physics and mathematics, then connect that motivation to documented experience deriving and implementing computational models. Emphasize reasoning from governing principles instead of treating simulation software as a black box. Do not imply that useful tools, experimental validation, or practical outcomes are unimportant; the candidate has not expressed such a tradeoff.

**Authentic phrasing options:**
- I am most interested in engineering work where I can build mathematical and physics-based models from first principles.
- What draws me to simulation engineering is the opportunity to start with the governing physics and develop a computational model that can explain and predict system behavior.
- I am particularly motivated by problems that require more than setting up an existing software workflow: defining the model, understanding its assumptions, and working through the numerical formulation.
- What I find most rewarding is understanding the physics and mathematics behind a system before deciding how to represent it computationally.
- I enjoy working through the governing principles of a problem and translating that understanding into a defensible simulation model.

**Avoid:** "I have always been passionate about physics"; "I prefer building everything from scratch"; "commercial software is unreliable". These claims were not established.

## MOT-002 — Connecting mathematical models with useful engineering tools

**Status:** Suggested interpretation; confirm before treating as personal motivation

**Possible statement:** I find it rewarding when a physical formulation becomes a computational tool that engineers can use to evaluate alternatives and make design decisions.

**Potential evidence to verify in experience libraries:** custom FEM solvers, real-time HVAC plant models, Python/CadQuery automation, CAD-to-mesh algorithms.

**Cover-letter use:** Optional until candidate confirms the motivation. Experience may still be discussed factually.

## MOT-003 — Model credibility and validation

**Status:** Suggested interpretation; confirm before treating as personal motivation

**Possible statement:** I care about whether a simulation represents real system behavior, not only whether it converges numerically.

**Potential evidence to verify:** experimental structural digital twin and modal model updating, fan-shroud prototype testing, laboratory drop testing, other simulation validation projects.

**Cover-letter use:** Optional until confirmed as a personal value. Do not claim completed validation for projects listed as in development.

## MOT-004 — Technical breadth and coupling across disciplines

**Status:** Suggested interpretation; confirm before treating as personal motivation

**Possible statement:** I enjoy engineering problems that require linking mechanics, fluid dynamics, thermal effects, controls, or software rather than treating each subsystem in isolation.

**Potential evidence to verify:** NASA MCVT, John Deere real-time HVAC plant simulation, coastal bridge fluid–structure interaction.

**Cover-letter use:** Optional until confirmed as a personal preference.

## MOT-005 — Work environment and role preferences

**Status:** Not established as personal motivation

**Questions for later confirmation:**
- Do I want to spend most of my time deriving methods, programming solvers, validating with experiments, or a balanced combination?
- Do I prefer scientific software/platform teams, advanced R&D, or product development with strong simulation ownership?
- What type of technical autonomy or collaboration matters most to me?

Do not write claims about preferred company culture, leadership style, location, or mission without explicit candidate input.

## MOT-006 — Company- and job-specific reasons

**Status:** Must be sourced per application

For each application, identify a specific technical responsibility, problem, or published company initiative that genuinely aligns with MOT-001 and the candidate's documented experience. If none is available, state an interest in the *work described in the position* rather than inventing allegiance to the employer.

## Writing voice

- First person, direct, curious, technically grounded, and restrained.
- Prefer specific problems, governing principles, computational choices, and validation to generic enthusiasm.
- Use a natural tone; no superlatives or unsupported emotional claims.
- Keep cover letters approximately 250–350 words, unless requested otherwise.
- Use 1–2 selected accomplishment IDs as concrete evidence; pull factual details from the original experience libraries.
- Distinguish **motivation** (this library) from **evidence** (achievement libraries) and **role fit** (job requirements).

## Hard authenticity rules

1. MOT-001 and its deeper emphasis on understanding underlying physics and mathematics are explicitly confirmed personal motivations. MOT-002 through MOT-005 remain unconfirmed.
2. The other themes are hypotheses to discuss with the candidate, not first-person beliefs to assert as fact.
3. Never invent an emotional connection to a company or its products.
4. Never claim tools or experience absent from the experience libraries.
5. Never imply patents were granted when only disclosures were submitted.
6. Preserve project status and quantitative baselines.
7. A cover letter may rewrite accomplishment phrasing for narrative flow, but technical facts must match the source.
8. Do not put unconfirmed motivations into a finished letter without candidate approval.
